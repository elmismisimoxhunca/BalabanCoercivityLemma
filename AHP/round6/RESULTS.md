## Probe findings

### Sign: positive on the requested grid, negative in the true small-amplitude regime

All 32 requested samples, with q equal to 1 or 2, R equal to 18, 20, 24 or 30, and amplitudes from one thousandth to one millionth, have positive correction on the numerically identified **actual first-crossing kernel**.

This is not uniform down to zero. For q=2, R=18 and amplitude \(10^{-100}\), the first-kernel correction is approximately

\[
\langle v,Dv\rangle=-1.8124680628969906869\times10^{-170}.
\]

Independent 400- and 500-digit calculations agree to 235.57 relative digits. A separate finite-volume vacuum calculation gives the negative leading coefficient

\[
c_1=-1.8124681040894913682\times10^{-70}.
\]

This is strong numerical evidence against the unchanged all-q `InitialKernelControl` route. It is not a formal whole-kernel expansion or a logical disproof from one negative sample. That premise asks for arbitrarily small successful amplitudes, and its sign clause asks for some positive direction in the exact real kernel.

The same-kernel pre-crossing test succeeds numerically on the requested grid. At the decisive tiny amplitude it instead gives a positive corrected form, approximately \(2.7187021\times10^{-170}\), so it supplies no negative test there. This does not exclude another vector, another crossing mechanism or another family. A negative test form would itself still be an ingredient of the intermediate-value argument, not a certificate that the corrected operator is exactly singular.

### Scaling: very small tails and nonuniform limits

- For q=1 the requested grid is approximately quadratic in amplitude, with radial decay \(\rho_0^{4R}\), where \(\rho_0=(3-\sqrt5)/2\). The fitted multiplier is about 0.0188582729848, and the exponential decay rate per R is 3.84969460048. At much smaller amplitude, mixing of the two boundary modes halves the multiplier; the sampled sign stays positive.
- For q=2 the positive grid is approximately linear in amplitude, with radial factor \((s\rho_k)^{8R}\), where \(s=3-2\sqrt2\), \(T=4(1-\cos(\pi/8))\), and \(\rho_k=(3+T-\sqrt{(3+T)^2-4})/2\). The fitted multiplier is about \(5.06749306069\times10^{-7}\); the decay rate per R is 22.8037297149. This is a pre-asymptotic law, not a positive small-amplitude theorem. Smaller amplitudes enter an intermediate negative inverse-amplitude regime, then an ultimately negative linear regime at fixed radius.

Taking amplitude to zero and increasing radius cannot be interchanged on the strength of these grid fits. The finite-volume left/right splitting matters.

### Structure: the first sector and the propagated source matter

The physical transverse symmetry is generated by two-site translations: \(C_{4q}\times C_{4q}\), not a replacement small torus. The first charged character is (0,0) for q=1, at angle approximately 0.58655896864, but (7,7) for q=2, at approximately 0.56397151799. The q=2 zero-character crossing is later; its provisional tests are not guarded witnesses. The first charged kernel is numerically simple over the complex numbers and therefore two-dimensional over the reals.

The probe uses analytic raw Taylor jets and full-carrier augmented inverses, rather than inverting a boundary compression or replacing D by a curvature mask. Its source propagates through the constrained inverse. The exterior term has a sine/cosine pencil identity; when B is positive definite, its rescaling gives a monotonicity argument before the first crossing. Flat-collar recurrences describe the tails. A tridiagonal neutral-source calculation gives an explicit finite hyperbolic-sine sum, whose contraction with the finite-volume vacuum kernel yields the negative coefficient above. These reductions have not been turned into a complete Lean operator/Fourier dictionary.

Initial B and B-D are numerically positive definite on the requested grid and in the decisive tiny example. Thus the observed sign reversal is not explained by a detected initial-positivity failure.
