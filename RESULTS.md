# Mathematical results

## Uniform coercivity

In three-dimensional SU(2) lattice gauge theory, the coercivity estimate holds uniformly over admissible depths and background configurations. The inverse operators required to define the estimate exist under the stated hypotheses. The constants are chosen before the cube factor, scales, depth, and torus; no explicit or sharp numerical values are asserted.

The assumptions include periodic aligned geometry, admissible towers and collars, local gauge regularity, site coverage, and common-plaquette coverage. The supporting constructions include local gauge charts and a covariant right inverse for multilevel averaging, with weighted norm and covariant-gradient bounds.

There are proper depth-two examples with nonzero curvature, a background that is not pure gauge, and a nonzero admissible field. These establish joint non-vacuity at depth two, not richness at every depth.

## Coverage

Removing both coverage conditions from the specified formulation gives a false statement, even when inverse existence is granted, for every \(\ell\geq2\), \(c\geq10\), and \(a>0\). Retaining both conditions gives a consequence of the coercivity theorem.

At \(\ell=2\), a depth-two example satisfies site coverage but violates common-plaquette coverage. Thus retaining only site coverage is insufficient in that formal class. These counterexamples concern the specified formulation, not every interpretation of Balaban’s hypotheses.

## Boundary conventions

The unguarded four-bond touching formulation is false for every \(\ell\geq2\) and \(0<a\leq4\sqrt5\). Its counterexamples use singular operators and totalised inverses; they do not establish failure when all required inverses exist.

Class-inclusion comparisons distinguish bonds with both endpoints in a set from bonds touching it, and distinguish interior plaquettes from plaquettes with a corner in the set. Proper-domain examples for the four-bond convention exist for every \(\ell\geq2\). These flat examples are distinct from the curved non-vacuity construction above.

## Normalization

Under the specified inverse-existence assumptions and linked conventions, the raw and normalized full forms agree with comparison scalar 1 and unchanged \(a\). This is an identity between defined forms, not a determination of every intended gauge or parametrization in the source.

Further results treat non-strict collars, alternative coordinate readings, scale conventions, a global gauge assumption, Hilbert–Schmidt normalization, and quantifier order.

## All-plaquette formulations

The all-plaquette formulation with the full set of inverse-existence assumptions is proved.

The weaker formulation assuming only existence of the first inverse remains formally open. Numerical evidence strongly disfavors it, but does not constitute a formal counterexample. A counterexample still requires identification of the model matrices with the physical operators, a nonzero-area argument on the same kernel generator, and control over the required cofinal range of scales.

These questions are separate from the coercivity theorem under interior-plaquette localization.
